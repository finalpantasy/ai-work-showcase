01 / Acquisition · new customers

A first order is only the start.

Objective Acquire first-time customers at a sustainable total cost.

Evidence in view

  • In this example, 64% of attributed orders are first purchases.
  • New-customer add-to-cart improved from 4.6% to 5.1%; completed-order contribution is still unknown.
  • The reported ROAS stops at a seven-day attribution view.

Still unknown

  • First-order contribution after product margin, shipping, and discounts.
  • Repeat-purchase value: no lifetime-value source is connected.
  • Conversions that arrive after the seven-day window.
Suggested next move
Let customer value mature before making a scale call.

Keep the test within its existing cap, then compare first-order contribution with mature repeat-purchase cohorts and longer-lag conversions.

Next evidence · margin · repeat purchases · attribution lag

02 / Cross-sell · existing customers

Measure the attach, not just the ad.

Objective Grow companion-product attachment among eligible existing customers.

Evidence in view

  • The reported ROAS reflects purchases across the full audience, not only eligible past buyers.
  • In the sample funnel, 8.6% add the companion item to cart; 2.9% complete checkout with it.
  • The brief is about an incremental product attach, not broad revenue alone.

Still unknown

  • How many buyers would attach the item without the offer.
  • Attach rate among eligible customers versus the full audience.
  • Contribution after the bundle discount and fulfillment cost.
Suggested next move
Test the offer with the people it is meant to serve.

Compare an eligible-customer bundle test with a suitable baseline. Read add-to-cart through paid order, then judge incremental attachment and contribution per eligible order.

Next evidence · eligible buyers · checkout completion · contribution

03 / New test · client-directed

Respect the test’s agreed runway.

Objective Learn whether a new creative concept can outperform the current control.

Client directionKeep the new test live through the agreed review date.

Evidence in view

  • The new concept is at the midpoint of its planned comparison; the seven-day ROAS is an early read.
  • Its seven-day reported ROAS is visible, while conversion credit can continue to arrive later.
  • The written brief asks to keep the test live through the agreed review date.

Still unknown

  • Whether the result holds at the pre-agreed maturity and sample checkpoint.
  • Completed-order quality and contribution after the attribution lag.
  • Whether the concept wins against its control once both have a fair read.
Suggested next move
Honor the client’s instruction, then review against the test plan.

Keep the test within the already approved cap through the stated date. At the checkpoint, compare mature conversion quality and contribution against the control before recommending whether to extend, revise, or stop.

Next evidence · agreed checkpoint · mature conversions · control comparison

Product direction / Personal fit

Many models.
One place to direct the work.

I use AI to build products, investigate problems, create assets, and run complex projects. As work spreads across conversations, the challenge is continuity: which project owns an output, which account is selected, what changed, what was approved, and what the next session needs to know.

I’m developing Summoner as a desktop AI harness for directing that work. It brings projects, conversations, tasks, context, outputs, model and account choices, and review into one workspace. I define the product direction and operating model, direct AI-assisted implementation, and review results on an open-source foundation.

It is designed around how I work: explicit choices, durable decisions, clear ownership, and visible checks. The aim is less time rebuilding project context and more time directing and assessing the work.

Current statusLive account and runtime integration in progress

The current sample shows account/model controls, context views, synthetic code review, and a user-triggered critique flow.

  1. 01

    Project continuity

    Projects connect conversations, tasks, drafts, and results; the sample keeps each chat bound to its chosen account and runtime identity.

  2. 02

    Inspectable context

    A memory-graph beta links project material with source previews, relevance, freshness, and missing-context indicators.

  3. 03

    Deliberate cross-model critique

    A fresh user action sends one selected piece of work for critique or fact-checking, with replies kept in a review thread.

  4. 04

    Review and recovery

    Synthetic task branches isolate changes; turn diffs and restore points support review; approval and commit stay separate, and changed files invalidate approval.

Read implementation boundaries

Account choice remains explicit. If the selected account is unavailable, the sample stops instead of silently switching. Model and effort controls in the current sample do not execute live inference.

The context graph shows source provenance and stale or missing material, with project and source-account boundaries applied before retrieval. Private-library integration remains a later step.

The cross-model relay is user-directed, uses a fresh click for each send, and preserves the review thread; live signed-in send acceptance is still pending. Code review currently uses synthetic repository data, and real-repository use remains gated.
