# A brief a designer can use

Fictional portfolio example. Juniper Supply, its product and all performance are invented. The advertisement is AI-generated demonstration material and has never run in an account.

## The job

Prepare two single-image concepts for people packing for a weekend walk. Compare two buying reasons while keeping the product, audience, format, offer and destination the same.

## The customer

Someone who enjoys a short hike but does not want planning it to become a project. They are choosing a reusable bottle and want the trip to feel easy to start.

## What we know

The sample image shows a bottle with a carry handle. It can support a visual concept about bringing the bottle along. We do not have verified evidence for temperature retention, leak resistance or durability. Do not put those claims in the ads.

## Two concepts

**Easy start:** Show the bottle beside the small set of things packed for a walk. Opening line: “A little less planning. A little more outside.” Explain the simple buying moment. Call to action: Explore the bottle.

**Small detour:** Use the supplied outdoor bottle image. Opening line: “Built for the detour.” Focus on making a short trip feel worthwhile. Call to action: Explore the bottle.

## Keep the test readable

The planned variable is the buying reason. Keep product, audience, placement, image format, destination and offer consistent. Delivery can still differ; this is a practical test plan, not a guarantee of a controlled experiment.

## What the designer hands back

- Two clearly named concepts with the bottle visible and short readable text.
- The requested placement sizes, checked for cropping.
- A note if the concept needs a product claim we cannot yet support.
- Source files and exports kept together with their concept names.

## Before launch

Check the copy, product claims, destination, format and asset ownership. Review the actual preview. Confirm the test question and what remains unchanged. A completed draft is not an approved launch.

## What we will learn

Review the delivery and business outcome over a stated period. Look at spend, purchases, return and the customer context. If there is little delivery, say that. If one concept performs better, use it as a direction for the next test; do not claim the message alone caused the difference.
