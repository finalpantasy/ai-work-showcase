Campaign Background: What problem did the Advertiser seek your Agency to solve?

Compartés is a Los Angeles chocolatier creating chocolate as art since 1950. A small team makes premium bars and gifts with bold flavor, high-quality ingredients and design-led packaging. The brand serves individual gift buyers and corporate clients. November and December are its most important sales period.

The business challenge. Holiday is the moment when Compartés' product, packaging and gifting relevance are strongest, and when every premium gift category competes hardest for the same shopper. The team needed to increase Meta investment quickly, protect purchase economics and drive orders before shipping cutoffs. The response was an account built for Meta's AI-first delivery environment, with broad audience freedom, open placements, deep creative choice and disciplined phase-based scaling.

Competitive landscape. Compartés is positioned above supermarket chocolate and with design-focused gifting brands. Packaging is a primary sales asset. Each bar has a collectible presentation.

Why Compartés engaged the team. The team manages paid social, paid search, SEO and creative strategy through one senior team. The same team can simplify Meta, coordinate channel pacing and evaluate platform results against the wider business.

Campaign Goal: What challenges did you aim to address by advertising with Meta for this specific campaign? What goals were you trying to achieve?

Primary business objective. Turn Meta into a dependable holiday growth engine. Compartés needed far more volume during its most important two months while keeping the account-level platform ROAS above the team's 4x operating threshold and checking every major budget decision against total business efficiency.

Peak-period ambition. The clearest test was Black Friday and Cyber Monday: nearly double Meta investment in the same weekday-aligned nine-day window and improve the economics at the same time. The result was 99.0% more spend, 104.7% more attributed purchase value, 120.7% more attributed purchases, 2.9% higher ROAS, 9.8% lower cost per purchase and 31.9% lower CPM.

Efficiency guardrails. The team and Compartés agreed on a platform ROAS threshold above 4x, overall business-efficiency targets and controlled 10% to 20% investment increases. This created room to scale quickly when the account could absorb more spend while protecting the commercial outcome.

Budget control. The team mapped approximately $200,000 across the season, reserved 40% to 50% for the peak window and paced each phase against the prior year's daily spend. Final Meta investment reached $201,211, within 0.6% of plan, and 40% landed in the peak period.

Measurement standard. Meta Ads Manager measured delivery and attributed purchase efficiency. Shopify measured store sales, orders, average order value and customer mix. Triple Whale cross-checked channel spend and customer outcomes. Together, the three systems showed how stronger Meta performance aligned with total-business growth.

Business outcome. Shopify recorded $2,843,922 in sales and 21,112 orders during the November–December window. First-time customer orders reached 11,800, up 42.1% year over year, while returning-customer orders grew 21.3%.

Meta product strategy. Use Advantage+ Audience, Advantage+ Placements, Advantage+ Shopping and Dynamic Product Ads as one connected growth system. Give Meta more eligible people, placements, products and creative to work with, while the team controls budget pace and commercial guardrails.

Account-wide demand creation. Purchase optimization remained the objective across Compartés' Meta account. Across evergreen, promotional, catalog and seasonal campaign roles, Holiday 2025 delivered 11,418,646 impressions, up 37.4% year over year, while CTR rose 40.3%. During the nine-day BFCM window, the account reached 896,301 people, up 163.9% year over year, as spend rose 99.0%. CPM fell 31.9%, while frequency increased only from 2.92 to 3.23. Across the full season, cost per click fell 17.1%, cost per add to cart fell 30.0% and cost per checkout fell 16.9%.

Operating discipline. Review performance against the season plan, increase budgets in measured steps and move spend only when return and purchase-cost signals support the next increase.

Campaign Approach / Solution: How did you address the challenges you faced? In what ways did you leverage Meta to achieve your campaign goals?

AI-first creative strategy. The team made creative the engine of Meta's delivery. The team continuously tested new concepts, creators, hooks, copy variations, product stories, formats and audience-specific messaging angles, then fed the strongest combinations back into broad Advantage+ delivery. Reliable purchase signals, open placements and catalog depth gave Meta room to learn, while the breadth and pace of the team's creative testing gave the system better ads to match with each shopper.

Technical strategy. The team paired a simpler account structure with Meta's automation. That gave the platform more room to match each creative with the right shopper and placement while giving the team a clear view of where budget was working hardest. Advantage+ Audience handled approximately 87% of consumer spend, Advantage+ Placements opened the full inventory, lowest-cost bidding preserved auction flexibility, and Advantage+ Shopping with Dynamic Product Ads expanded the product and creative pool. The team kept commercial control through phase-based budgets, measured 10% to 20% increases and a 4x platform ROAS guardrail.

The account applied Meta's Performance 5 principles across five connected levers:

1. Account simplification. The team gave every campaign one clear job: always-on sales, BFCM promotion, catalog-driven product discovery or seasonal launches. This reduced overlap, kept purchase data from being scattered across too many places and made it easier to move budget toward the strongest opportunity.

2. AI-powered automation. Advantage+ Audience and broad delivery expanded the system's search space. Advantage+ Placements opened Feed, Stories, Reels and the wider inventory. Purchase optimization and lowest-cost bidding let Meta respond to demand at impression level.

3. Continuous creative testing. The team kept the account supplied with new ideas throughout the season. UGC from at least seven named creators, multi-creator compilations, holiday statics, dynamic GIFs, designed catalog ads and seasonal product stories tested distinct hooks, copy, formats and shopper motivations. Assets across all placement sizes gave Meta a deep pool of options across Feed, Stories and Reels. The team moved spend toward the strongest combinations and used each result to shape the next round of creative.

4. Signal quality. Meta Pixel and Conversions API supplied browser and server-side purchase signals, with Purchase Event Match Quality reaching 9.3. Catalog data, Shopify Audiences, purchaser exclusions, a high-LTV Klaviyo segment and selective lookalikes added first-party guidance across acquisition and retargeting.

5. Results validation. Meta Ads Manager measured attributed purchase performance, Shopify confirmed store growth and customer mix, and Triple Whale cross-checked spend and customer outcomes. The team used all three views to protect the business case as investment increased.

The system connected four commercial growth engines:

Demand capture. $121,805.49, or 60.5% of Meta spend, returned 5.29x. Broad delivery carried most of the volume, while a selective household-income and Online Shopping test with Advantage+ Placements returned 7.12x, the strongest result among scaled ad sets.

Black Friday and Cyber Monday acceleration. Against the weekday-aligned 2024 window, Meta investment rose 99.0%, purchase value rose 104.7%, purchases rose 120.7%, ROAS improved 2.9%, cost per purchase fell 9.8% and CPM fell 31.9%. A UGC compilation absorbed $9,466 at 4.65x, while closing urgency returned 4.76x.

Automated product discovery. Advantage+ Shopping spent $22,820.86 at 3.44x. Designed feed treatments carried Compartés' visual identity into catalog delivery, and Dynamic Product Ads accounted for $18,438 with 2.09% and 2.19% CTRs, the strongest format-level click-through rates in the account.

Seasonal product launches. Dedicated sales activity supported limited-edition bars and the advent calendar. Creative built around scarcity and gifting intent produced CTRs of 2.5% to 4.4% against a 1.375% account average.

Creative learning loop. Concepts launched in named batches and fixed windows. Meta could concentrate delivery around stronger ads while the team retained clean comparisons across concepts. Holiday Round 2 returned 5.79x versus 5.02x for Round 1, and Chocolate of the Month Club earned additional budget after returning 4.79x.

Human control at holiday speed. Meta's models handled person, placement and creative matching at impression level. The team controlled the commercial decisions: which concepts entered testing, when budgets increased, where spend moved and how investment responded to agreed efficiency guardrails. The season plan finished at $201,211, within 0.6% of the approximately $200,000 target.

What made the work different.

AI fluency became operating discipline. The team gave Meta's retrieval and ranking systems a larger, better-organized set of audience, product, placement and creative options, then used human judgment to govern pace and risk. During BFCM, spend rose 99.0% while ROAS improved 2.9%, cost per purchase fell 9.8% and CPM fell 31.9%.

Creative strengthened delivery. The account used varied concepts, formats and buyer cues as matching inputs for Meta's AI. UGC, statics, GIFs, catalog treatments and seasonal stories gave the system multiple routes to relevance across Feed, Stories and Reels.

Cross-source business validation. Meta-attributed purchases and purchase value rose as investment scaled. Shopify recorded 33.1% sales growth, 32.1% order growth and 42.1% growth in first-time customer orders. Triple Whale closely matched Shopify order and customer counts and Meta spend, confirming that the growth pattern held across all three reporting views.

Targeting: Who was your target audience? What Meta tools or strategies did you use to reach them?

Primary audience. US shoppers ages 18 to 65 buying premium gifts under a holiday delivery deadline. Purchase optimization allowed Meta's delivery system to identify intent from behavior, creative response, product interest and conversion signals.

Audience architecture. Advantage+ Audience covered approximately 87% of consumer spend across holiday, Black Friday, catalog and seasonal product activity. The team kept eligibility broad so Meta could use real-time purchase, product and creative-response signals without fragmenting learning across narrow audience groups.

First-party guidance. Shopify Audiences supported prospecting and retargeting alongside Existing Customers Plus, a high-LTV Klaviyo segment, a 1% purchaser lookalike and 90-day and 180-day purchaser exclusions. These inputs guided delivery while age, location and exclusion controls protected the campaign's commercial boundaries.

Selective precision. Select evergreen ad sets retained household-income signals. One combined Online Shopping with Advantage+ Placements and returned 7.12x, the strongest result among scaled ad sets. The team kept signal-led tests where account economics supported them.

Creative-driven relevance. UGC, catalog treatments, gifting scenes, offer creative and product stories carried distinct buyer cues. Those differences gave Meta's models richer creative representations and more ways to match the message to the shopper across Feed, Stories and Reels.

Audience testing. Signal-led tests ran in separate ad sets with clean spend and return visibility. Lookalike and subscription tests used the same structure, giving the team a disciplined way to challenge broad and Advantage+ delivery without fragmenting the core account.

How were these results measured?

  • Meta Ads Manager: primary source for spend, impressions, reach, frequency, clicks, CPM, CPC, CTR, attributed purchases and attributed conversion value. Data was pulled at account, campaign, ad set, ad and placement level for both windows.
  • Attribution model: 7-day click and 1-day view, applied consistently in both years. Reporting uses Meta's omni purchase metric throughout the submission.
  • Shopify: primary source for orders, revenue, average order value and first-time versus returning customer mix. Reports used were Sales over time, Average order value over time, First-time versus returning customer sales and the customer cohort report. Each report covered both windows with year-over-year comparison enabled.
  • Google Ads: spend and conversion data pulled directly from the Google Ads API.
  • Meta Pixel and Conversions API: supported browser and server-side purchase measurement. Purchase Event Match Quality reached 9.3 for Purchase.
  • Triple Whale: used as a cross-check. Primary reporting uses Shopify and platform data. Triple Whale matched Shopify orders within 0.55%, first-time orders within 0.40% and Meta spend within 0.66%.
  • Comparison design. The Black Friday windows were weekday aligned. Meta attribution settings and the omni purchase definition remained constant, and Shopify reports used the same year-over-year date comparison.
  • Decision framework. The team read platform efficiency and total-store growth together when deciding whether to increase budgets. That kept live optimization tied to commercial performance throughout the season.
