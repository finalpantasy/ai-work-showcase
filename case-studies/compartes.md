# Compartés: AI-assisted strategy for a demanding holiday campaign

**Context:** 2025 holiday campaign, recognized in the 2026 Meta Agency Awards. **My role:** Helped lead the team, combining performance strategy with AI-assisted creative, copy, analysis and workflow tooling. **Outcome ownership:** Team result.

## The commercial problem

Holiday marketing has a compressed decision window. The team needed to grow demand while increasing advertising investment and maintaining efficiency. My contribution brought together marketing judgment and regular AI-assisted work: developing creative and copy, examining performance, and improving tooling that supports decisions.

The useful question is where AI helped the team prepare and iterate, and where people retained accountability. Creative selection, budgets and execution remained human responsibilities. This case does not claim an experiment measuring AI's incremental effect.

## My AI-assisted contribution

I helped direct creative and copy development, analysis and supporting tooling. My contribution connected AI-assisted output to a marketing decision: what proposition to communicate, how to assess an asset, what evidence an interpretation needs and what the team should review next.

I view AI drafts as material for review. A fluent draft can make an unsupported claim, repeat an angle without adding a useful test, or misread a comparison period. Marketing expertise turns that material into a usable brief and a defensible recommendation. The campaign outcome belongs to the team; this describes my contribution.

## A practical decision framework

The following illustrates my operating approach. It explains judgment, rather than reconstructing a log of individual campaign actions.

### Creative: require a testable proposition

A useful brief connects a customer motivation, a product truth and a specific message. Variation should change something meaningful, such as the opening proposition or reason to buy. Rewording the same idea many times is not a new strategy. AI helps explore candidates; the reviewer checks claims, relevance and the quality of the test.

### Budget: connect a reference to context

Pacing explains spend against a plan. It cannot decide the right budget by itself. During a promotion, demand and inventory can make a linear plan a poor description of what should happen each day. I want visible arithmetic and a decision that considers efficiency, constraints and the promotional calendar.

The portfolio calculator uses fictional data to make that distinction concrete. Its daily reference is a planning calculation, not an instruction to change an account.

### Measurement: preserve the question and the period

Platform-attributed purchases answer a different question from total-store sales. More purchases can reflect more spending, better efficiency or both. I separate investment, volume and efficiency, retain comparison windows and avoid attributing a team outcome to one tool. This matters when favorable metrics could otherwise be combined into an exaggerated story.

## Why the results matter

The nine-day report describes higher attributed purchase volume alongside improved reported efficiency as investment grew. The store report describes a separate sales and first-customer outcome. They provide campaign evidence, but neither establishes that every sale was incremental or that AI caused the lift.

For an AI strategy role, the relevant experience is bringing AI into an accountable process and contributing to a recognized commercial campaign. It is stronger than listing tools while preserving honest attribution.
