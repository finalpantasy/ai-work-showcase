A personal brain for my work

My role: Designed and built a private knowledge system and the rules behind how I use AI. The work records and source material stay private.

Why I built it

I work across marketing, product ideas, team decisions and software projects. Useful knowledge gets buried in chats, documents and old tasks. Repeating the background wastes time. Losing an earlier decision can send a project in circles.

I built a personal brain to keep that knowledge usable. It holds work history, projects, decisions, skills, feedback and ways of working. I can find the relevant record and use it to help with the next task.

Give AI a useful memory

The system keeps links between a claim and the evidence behind it. A project record says what happened, when it happened, what I contributed and what is still uncertain.

That matters when I ask AI to help with a resume, a plan or an old project. It can look up the supporting work instead of filling the gaps with a confident story.

New material goes into a review queue. It becomes a trusted record after checking the source, duplicates, conflicts and privacy. Private details stay separate from material that is safe to share.

Save how I work, too

The brain includes operating rules based on my decisions. One example is how I explain results: lead with what happened, show the key evidence, say what we are doing and make the decision clear.

Other rules cover checking evidence before acting, reviewing live account changes and finding the real limit on growth. These help future AI work start with the standards I already use.

Make it easy to browse

I use Obsidian as the human interface. It has views for projects, career evidence, skills, recent records and items that need review. A search layer helps AI retrieve the right documents. Version history tracks reviewed material.

This is a working private system, with different levels of coverage across its sources. It does not remember everything automatically. Source checks and unresolved gaps remain visible.

What this shows about me

I can take a messy information problem and build a system around it. I think about how information comes in, how it gets checked, how someone finds it and how it helps them act.

The result is a foundation for my own work with AI: shared context, clearer handoffs and less dependence on remembering which chat had the answer.
